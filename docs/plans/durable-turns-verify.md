# Verification: durable-turns.md (research checkpoint partial fix)

Status: verification report (no implementation). Adversarial review of
`docs/plans/durable-turns.md` against source, the incident doc, and the
Cloudflare `cloudflare` skill references. Evidence is `file:line`, doc citations,
and the CF limits tables.

## Verdict

The plan is well-researched and its Cloudflare facts are accurate. But it has
**one blocker** that undermines the fix's core mechanism, and its motivating
cost numbers are **stale**, which shrinks the value case by roughly an order of
magnitude. As written it would not reliably deliver Maria's answer on the retry.

- The turn key depends on the model regenerating a **byte-identical research
  prompt** on the retry. Sampling temperature is the Anthropic default (1.0);
  no `temperature` is set anywhere (`rg temperature apps/agent-api/src` → empty).
  So the retry produces a different prompt → different `promptHash` → **cache
  miss** → research re-runs from scratch. The fix delivers nothing in the common
  case. **Blocker.**
- The "10-minute, ~$3 redo" the plan repeatedly cites is **pre-fix** behavior.
  Commit `131a7f0` (stripped write tools from research) plus the sibling pipeline
  fixes already cut a research turn to **~88s end-to-end, ~48s research, ~$0.22**
  (measured, bottom of `agent-latency-investigation.md`). A reset mid-research
  now loses ~48s and a few cents, not 10 min and $3. **Concern** that reframes
  "is it worth it".

Recommendation below: fix the key or don't ship; and given the shrunken benefit,
ship the **observability slice alone first** to measure how often a reset even
lands during research before building the cache.

## Would the partial fix have saved Maria's answer?

Traced concretely against the incident (reset surfaced at `persistReply` in
`interface.ts` final delivery, after research completed):

1. **First attempt: unchanged — she still gets the fallback.** With the
   checkpoint in place, research completes ~1 min in and `putTurnCache(report)`
   commits to SQLite (a real durable write; a later reset does not roll it back).
   The turn continues to final delivery, the deploy's reset hits the next SQLite
   write (`persistReply`), the turn-level `catch` in `orchestrator.ts` sends
   `FALLBACK_MESSAGE` over Telegram (a `fetch`, unaffected). Maria sees the
   fallback on attempt 1 regardless. The plan says this plainly; it is correct.
2. **Retry: correct *only if* the model reproduces the same research prompt.**
   The alarm re-fires, `runTurn` re-reads history (tail is still `user` because
   `persistReply` never committed), `runInterfaceAgent` runs the model from
   scratch. The model *should* call `research` again — but at temperature 1.0 the
   research `prompt` string it emits will differ token-for-token, so
   `promptHash` differs and the cache **misses**. Research then re-runs the full
   (~48s post-fix) loop. Only if the prompt happens to hash identically does the
   cache hit and she gets the real answer in seconds.

**Honest answer:** the first attempt is a fallback either way; the retry would
have delivered her real answer *only under a key that actually rehits*, which the
plan's `promptHash` key does not guarantee. As specified, the retry most likely
re-runs research (pre-fix behavior for the redo cost, just shorter now). Fix the
key (below) and the answer becomes: **yes, fast and correct on the retry.**

## Findings (ranked)

### Blocker

**B1. The `promptHash` turn key will miss on the retry (defeats the fix).**
The plan keys the research cache on `(conversationId, tailUserMessageId,
promptHash)` and assumes "the retry answers the same tail user message, so a
cache keyed on it is stable." That is true for `conversationId` and
`tailUserMessageId`, but **not** for `promptHash`: the research prompt is
generated by the interface model, and `model.ts` sends no `temperature`
(Anthropic default 1.0; `MAX_TOKENS`/`REQUEST_TIMEOUT_MS` are set, temperature is
not — `apps/agent-api/src/agents/model.ts:52,57,146`). A fresh `runInterfaceAgent`
on the retry regenerates the prompt nondeterministically, so `promptHash` changes
and the cache misses. The plan treats `promptHash` purely as collision-avoidance
(Risks → "Turn-key collision") and never confronts the inverse: it also makes the
retry miss. Since the entire value of the fix is "the retry hits the cache," this
is fatal as written.
- Fix options: (a) drop `promptHash`; key on `(conversationId, userMessageId)`
  and serve the cached report for the turn's research call, accepting
  **one research report per turn** (the incident and the measured turns are all
  single-research-call). Guard the multi-call case by storing a small list and
  serving by call ordinal, or by refusing to cache when a turn issues a second
  research call. (b) Make the research prompt deterministic enough to reproduce
  (e.g. temperature 0 for the interface agent, or derive the research prompt
  from the user message rather than free model text) — a broader change with its
  own quality tradeoffs. Option (a) is the contained one and should be the plan's
  default; the plan must pick a key that does not depend on prompt reproduction.

### Concern

**C1. Motivating cost is stale; the benefit is ~10x smaller than stated.**
The plan's justification ("converts a 10-min, $3 redo into seconds") is the
pre-fix incident. The same repo already fixed the research pipeline: `131a7f0`
(strip write tools) and siblings, with a **measured** post-fix turn of ~88s
total / ~48s research / $0.217 (`agent-latency-investigation.md`, "Post-fix
measurement"). A reset mid-research now discards ~48s and cents. The checkpoint
still removes a redo, but the cost/benefit that justified a new table, migration,
store methods on both adapters, and orchestrator→interface→research threading was
computed against a workload that no longer exists. The plan should re-derive the
benefit from post-fix numbers and state it honestly; at ~48s the case for
building this now is weak.

**C2. "Clear on reply commit" can wipe a freshly-cached report mid-turn.**
The research tool prompt instructs the interface agent to "acknowledge the user
with reply first," so a turn emits `reply(ack)` → `research` (caches) → possibly
another intermediate `reply` → final answer. The plan clears the cache "when the
turn's reply row is persisted (tail becomes `assistant`)" via
`clearTurnCache(conversationId)`, which wipes **all** entries for the
conversation. If any `reply` commits *after* research caches but *before* the
reset (any post-research intermediate reply), the report is cleared and the retry
re-runs research. Clearing must fire only at **turn completion** (after the final
delivery / writer), not on every `persistReply`, and ideally scope to the turn's
`userMessageId` rather than the whole conversation.

**C3. The staged production deploy-during-turn test is neither isolated nor
reliably reproducible.** Acceptance criterion 6 and the "Production verification"
section force a deploy while a test-chat turn is mid-research. But a deploy resets
**every** `UserDO` on that version, including real users mid-turn — you cannot
scope a deploy to one test DO. Gradual rollout (which the plan recommends) makes
it nondeterministic *which* DOs flip on a given step, so landing the reset on the
target DO within its ~1–2 min window is racy; the plan admits "repeat the deploy
within the window if the first does not land on that DO." So criterion 6 is a
best-effort, production-affecting, flaky probe, not an objectively checkable gate.
Keep it as an optional manual confidence check, but base acceptance on the
simulated-reset unit test (fresh agent objects, same `Store`, same key), which is
deterministic and honest on this box.

**C4. TTL sweep is unspecified; abandoned turns accumulate.** DO SQLite has no
auto-expiry. The plan says "defensively TTL rows (createdAt)" but names no sweep
trigger. Without a concrete sweep (e.g. delete `createdAt < now-1h` on each
`putTurnCache` or turn start), a turn that resets and is never retried leaves its
row forever. Specify the sweep, or the "what stops it accumulating" question is
unanswered.

### Nit

**N1. The turn key is described three inconsistent ways.** Goal/idempotency:
`(conversationId, tailUserMessageId)`; Recommendation: `(conversationId,
tailUserMessageId, promptHash)`; Ordered steps table: `turn_cache(conversationId,
userMessageId, kind, key=promptHash)`. Pick one and use it throughout (the fix
for B1 will settle it).

**N2. Cache value size not bounded.** The 8000-char ceiling
(`MAX_RESEARCH_RESULT_CHARS`, `interface.ts`) is applied only when rendering the
transcript, not to the report string the tool returns or would cache. State the
stored size bound explicitly (the report is small in practice, but the plan's
"how large" claim should be grounded, not implied).

## Point-by-point on the requested checks

1. **Cloudflare claims — all verified, no correction.**
   - DO reset on deploy is unavoidable, no drain / version-pin / finish-alarm
     hook: matches the DO reference (in-memory state lost on eviction/restart;
     reconstruct from storage) and the plan's known-issues / troubleshooting /
     versions-and-deployments citations. `do/alarm.ts:19-22` independently
     confirms the team already knows the reset "tears down the isolate before
     catch runs."
   - Persisted storage survives a reset: confirmed (DO troubleshooting; the whole
     fix relies on it and it is sound — a mid-turn `putTurnCache` commits and is
     not rolled back by a later reset).
   - Gradual deployments only shrink/stretch the window: correct.
   - Workflows facts all check out against the CF skill limits table
     (`references/workflows/gotchas.md`): **1 MiB** per step return (:32,:65),
     **1,024** steps per instance (:67), **10 min** default per-attempt step
     timeout (:73), completed steps skipped (step name = cache key; README:20,
     gotchas:23), 30-day paid retention (:72). No overclaim.
   - One nuance the plan already states correctly: an **in-flight** LLM call at
     reset time re-runs under any scheme (Workflows included); only a *completed*
     step's return is durable. Accurate.

2. **Does the partial fix fix Maria's failure?** Conditionally — see the traced
   answer above. First attempt: fallback (unchanged). Retry: correct only if the
   cache rehits, which B1 shows it does not reliably do. Fix B1 and it does.

3. **The turn key.** `conversationId` (UUID per `(chatId,topicId)`,
   `db.ts:getOrCreateConversation`) and the tail `userMessageId` (autoincrement
   PK, `schema.ts messages.id`) are both **stable across reset and retry**: the
   user row is persisted in `enqueueTurn` (`UserDO/index.ts:98`) before any turn
   runs, and the retry re-reads the same tail row. No cross-user collision (users
   are separate DOs; ids are per-DO). No cross-turn collision (a re-sent identical
   message is a new row with a new id). **The `promptHash` component is the only
   unstable part** and it breaks retry rehit (B1). Also note
   `getConversationHistory` (`db.ts`) drops the row `id`; the plan's step 2 to
   expose it is required and correct.

4. **Replay safety.** Confirmed read-only on a cache **hit**: the research tool
   only merges topic names into an in-memory `accessed` set and returns text
   (`tools/research.ts`); on a hit nothing calls the model, `web_search`
   (Brave), or `read_page`, so no rate-limit counters, no external I/O, no
   writes. Commit `131a7f0` removed the write tools (verified), so an in-loop
   topic write is structurally impossible. Logging is not state. Staleness
   relative to a changed question is a non-issue: a changed question is a new
   `userMessageId`, hence a different key. The only mutation on the **miss**
   (first) path is the intended `putTurnCache` checkpoint. Replay safety holds.

5. **Storage cost and lifetime.** Lives in DO SQLite (`turn_cache` table) —
   answered. Size — **not** bounded explicitly (N2); small in practice. Deleted
   on reply commit + `resetConversation` + TTL — but the reply-commit trigger is
   mis-scoped (C2) and the TTL sweep is unspecified (C4). So two of the four
   sub-questions are only partly answered.

6. **Cheap fix vs local optimum.** The failure mode is generic (any completed
   expensive work lost to a reset); the interface agent's own generation is also
   lost but is cheap (~5s) to redo. The plan is right that research was the one
   unit worth checkpointing — **when it cost 10 min / $3**. Post-fix (C1) that
   unit costs ~48s, so the special case buys much less while still entrenching a
   research-specific cache, its clear/TTL lifecycle, and threading that a general
   per-step checkpoint would later delete. Honest opinion: this is now a **weak
   local optimum**. It does not paint the codebase into a corner (the table can
   be generalized or dropped), but at ~48s of saved redo the marginal value may
   not clear the bar of a migration + dual-adapter store methods + cross-layer
   threading. If durability is genuinely wanted, the higher-value move is the one
   the incident's own "fix directions" name: make research **asynchronous from
   the reply** (ack, deliver when done), which removes both the latency *and* the
   10-min reset target, rather than caching a now-fast synchronous loop.

7. **Acceptance criteria.** Criteria 1–5 and 7 are objectively checkable by unit
   tests against the contract-tested `Store` and log spies — good. Criterion 6
   (staged deploy during a turn) is **not** safe or reproducible (C3): it resets
   real users' DOs and cannot be reliably aimed at the test DO. Demote it to an
   optional manual check; anchor acceptance on the deterministic simulated-reset
   unit test.

## Recommendation

Do **not** ship as written — B1 makes the retry miss the cache in the common
case, so the fix delivers little. Two viable paths:

1. **If you still want the checkpoint:** fix B1 first (key on
   `(conversationId, userMessageId)` with single-report-per-turn semantics, no
   `promptHash`), fix C2 (clear at turn completion only), specify C4's sweep, and
   re-derive the benefit from post-fix numbers (C1). Then it is a small, correct
   fix that makes the retry fast and correct. But weigh it against the ~48s it now
   saves.

2. **Preferred given C1:** ship the **observability slice alone first** — the
   `research`-during-reset counter / `turn_reset_recovered`-style signal — to
   measure how often a reset actually lands mid-research now that research is
   ~88s, not ~15 min. That is the lowest-risk, highest-information step and
   decides whether *any* checkpoint is worth building. If the signal shows the
   reset-during-research event is now rare (likely, given the shrunken window and
   the already-shipped scoped Workers Builds watch paths), defer the cache
   entirely and, if durability still matters, invest in async research decoupling
   or a general per-step checkpoint rather than a research-specific cache whose
   justifying workload no longer exists.

Do **not** adopt Workflows now — the plan's reasoning there is sound and its
facts check out.
